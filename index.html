Your hands are the instrument

The camera tracks 21 joints on each hand and strings a glowing thread between your fingertips. Nothing is recorded or uploaded — tracking runs entirely in this page.

How to play

  1. Raise both hands in front of the camera, palms facing it, about an arm's length away. Threads appear between matching fingertips.
  2. Stretch or shrink a thread by moving your hands apart or together — longer is lower, shorter is higher, just like a real string.
  3. Flick a fingertip quickly to pluck. The faster the flick, the louder the note, and sparks run along the thread.
  4. With one hand, threads fan out from your thumb to each fingertip instead — open and close your hand to change pitch, flick to play.
  5. No camera? The touch harp gives you ten strings on screen: press and drag across them, tap them, or use keys A–;.

How it works

Tracking. MediaPipe's hand landmarker runs in your browser and finds 21 joints per hand, dozens of times a second. The camera feed never leaves the page.

Pitch. Each thread's length is measured every frame and mapped onto the scale you picked — the same physics as a harp string, where length sets the note.

Sound. A pluck is synthesized live with the Karplus-Strong algorithm, which models a vibrating string by feeding a burst of noise through a short feedback loop — no audio files involved.

Tips. Good light on your hands and a plain background track best. If the camera is blocked or unavailable, the touch harp still plays the full instrument.
